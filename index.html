01 REPORTING

Rebuilding LAS reporting

I created Omni’s LAS reporting software so users could generate depth-bounded reports across multiple drilling runs, choose the curves and well context they needed, include all sorts of data, and merge gamma-memory measurements into the final output.

I also worked heavily on performance. Reporting more than 10,000 metres of well data had taken about 25 seconds in the previous software. My implementation brought the average generation time down to roughly 6 seconds.

03 FIELD WORKFLOW

PRIME API and synchronization

I worked on both sides of the API connection between PRIME and Omni. In redline-omni-api, I added authenticated ASP.NET Core endpoints, Entity Framework queries, and response models to expose PRIME data in the structure Omni needed. In the desktop app, I added client methods to make bearer-authenticated requests, deserialize the responses, map PRIME IDs and related entities into Omni’s domain models, and persist them through the local repository and Entity Framework layer.

I also worked in the job-sync pipeline itself, mapping PRIME responses back into Omni objects such as jobs, runs, tools, well legs, and related data while keeping their PRIME identities intact.

02 · EMBEDDED

STM32 Temperature
Display

I programmed an STM32 in C and wired it to a temperature sensor and an 8×8 LED matrix. I drove the display directly instead of using a dedicated matrix driver, so the firmware had to control the rows and columns itself.

The display used row scanning to light one row at a time at high speed. By updating the column values and cycling through the rows quickly enough, persistence of vision makes the separate flashes appear as one continuous image. I used that system to scroll the temperature reading across the matrix.
